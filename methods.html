Where the reviews came from

I collected the most recent public reviews for each app on : up to 500 per country (US and Canada) from the App Store's public review feed, and up to 1,500 from Google Play. That gives reviews in total. The case study uses reviews written between and .

I have no Google Play reviews for onX Offroad, so every Gaia vs onX comparison uses App Store reviews for both. The App Store feed only returns the newest 500 reviews per country. onX gets so many reviews that its US feed only reaches back to , so the Gaia vs onX comparison is limited to that date onward, the period both feeds fully cover. Other figures use the full window.

How reviews were sorted by type of complaint

For each type of complaint (the price, plus six points in a trip from planning a route to sharing it afterwards) I wrote a list of phrases (a regular expression) and matched it against the title and text of every review. A review can match several types, or none. Only 1 and 2-star reviews count as complaints.

To check the matching, I read a random sample of up to 20 matched 1 and 2-star reviews for each type, from Gaia and onX together, and counted how many were really about that type. When a type scored below 80%, I tightened its phrases and checked again.

Other phrase lists

Long-time or paying user: the reviewer describes their own use or payment over time, such as "I've used it for years", "subscribed for five years" or "loved it for a long time".
Hand check: of sampled matches were really long-time or paying users.

Mentions off-roading: words such as "off-road", "overland", "Jeep", "4x4", "dirt bike", "forest service road" or "BLM".
In-vehicle terms such as CarPlay and Android Auto are left out, so in-vehicle complaints can't make a review count as off-road by themselves.

How quotes were chosen and checked

I picked each quote by hand to illustrate a pattern the numbers already show, never to stand in for a number. Each quote is stored with its review ID, and the build script stops with an error if the quoted words don't appear exactly in that review. Quotes keep the reviewer's own spelling and punctuation. Reviewer names are never shown.

Other sources

Reddit. Posts and comments from r/GaiaGPS and from off-road, overlanding and BC subreddits that mention Gaia, onX or Trailforks, collected through the Arctic Shift archive of public Reddit data. Usernames are dropped when saved. Reddit has no star ratings, so a post counts as a complaint of a given type only when problem language ("doesn't work", "crash", "lost", "broken") appears within about 150 characters of that type's phrases. Hand-checked precision was lower than for app reviews: see the data build. The archive refused text searches in several off-road and BC subreddits even with slow retries, and capped Gaia comments in r/overlanding at the newest 100. So the Gaia vs onX Reddit comparison uses only r/overlanding, over the dates both searches fully cover ( to ).

Gaia's community forum. The newest public posts at help.gaiagps.com, read on . Each one shown on these pages is linked to its source.

BC off-road Facebook groups. Groups I'm a member of, searched for app names using Facebook's own group search, limited to posts since . I read relevant threads across groups. Because the groups are members-only, I recorded no names and no verbatim text, and these pages only summarise themes. Facebook search returns few posts per query, so this is a small sample used for direction only.

Help pages. The shared Outside account sign-in for Gaia GPS and Trailforks comes from Trailforks' public help page, checked on the same date.

What this data can't tell you, and what would

  • Who writes reviews. Unhappy users write more reviews, and many apps ask happy users for a star rating without a written review. Reviews compare which problems apps have. They don't measure overall satisfaction or how many users leave. What would: cancellation rates after reliability failures, from Outside's own data.
  • Phrase matching makes mistakes. Sampled precision was 80% to 94% per type, and reviews with unusual wording are missed. Counts are approximate: some false matches, some misses. What would: support-ticket categories, or a language model classifying every review with the same hand check.
  • Small numbers. Some onX complaint types have only a handful of reviews, so single-type ratios show direction only. The combined reliability figure rests on Gaia and onX reviews, so the ratio has a 95% confidence interval of to . Even the low end is a large gap. What would: the same comparison on app telemetry, which has no sample-size problem.
  • No internal data. Telemetry, cancellation reasons and conversion rates would outweigh everything here. The case study ends with the checks I'd run first and the result that would change the plan.
