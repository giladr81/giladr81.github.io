How I work

  • Start with customers and the people who talk to them every day.Before changing Cisco Investigate, I spoke with customers directly, from universities to large enterprises, and worked with our research team on more customer calls and recordings. I also interviewed about 40 customer success managers, account managers and support staff. One finding shaped the redesign: before blocking a suspicious domain, analysts needed to know what blocking it would break, so I made sure that information was the first thing they saw. After launch, usage analytics showed analysts opening Investigate from the console as part of their regular investigations, not just once.
  • Design for the strictest customer first.At AWS, some customers, in regulated industries for example, needed application discovery to run offline. I chose an offline-first design, which meant giving up the telemetry engineering relies on to see failures. I worked out a path with our principal engineer: offline first, then opt-in telemetry that customers control. I set up a support runbook to cover the gap.
  • Make the impact case before the build.On the internal platform for AWS's Migration Acceleration Program, the program team asked for fully automated customer onboarding. I walked them through impact and cost, prioritized a self-service progress view for customers, and launched a small version of the automation first.
